What is Spectrum

The event

Spectrum

Spectrum is the flagship photography event organized by the Photography Club of St. Sylvester’s College, Kandy. The event begins with an island-wide online photography competition, where participants submit their photographs through a Google Form under various categories. The selected and winning photographs are then showcased at a photography exhibition held at the college premises during the main event.

The Photography Day features an exhibition, an interactive session conducted by two renowned professional photographers serving as the Chief Guest and Guest of Honour, and a certificate award ceremony recognizing the competition winners. Souvenirs are also presented to the distinguished guests, the Principal, the MIC, and past presidents of the Photography Club. Through this event, Spectrum aims to celebrate creativity, encourage young photographers across Sri Lanka, and promote the art of photography within the school community.

Competition

Online Photography Competition

The Spectrum Online Photography Competition is an island-wide inter-school photography competition open to students from schools across Sri Lanka. The competition features five categories: Colour, Monochrome, Nature, Wildlife, and Mobile photography. Each participant may submit up to three photographs in each category.

The competition commenced on 14 August and will remain open until 30 September (extended from 20 September). A total of eight winners will be selected, including 1st place, 2nd place, 3rd place, and five merit award recipients. The winning and selected photographs will be displayed at the Spectrum Photography Exhibition, and the winners will be recognized during the certificate award ceremony held on the main Photography Day.

Exhibition

Photography Exhibition

The Spectrum Photography Exhibition is the main highlight of Spectrum Photography Day, held on 13 November 2026 at the Open Hall of St. Sylvester’s College, Kandy. The exhibition will feature the winning and selected photographs from the Spectrum island-wide inter-school photography competition, as chosen by the official judging panel. Students, participants, guests, and visitors will have the opportunity to view the exhibition throughout the event, celebrating the creativity and talent of young photographers from across Sri Lanka.

Main event

The Photography Day

The Spectrum Photography Day will be held on 13 November at the Cingolani Memorial Hall, St. Sylvester’s College, Kandy. The event will serve as the main celebration of Spectrum, bringing together students, participants, and distinguished guests for a day dedicated to the art of photography.

The programme will feature a photography exhibition showcasing selected and winning photographs from the island-wide online competition, educational lectures and interactive sessions conducted by two renowned professional photographers serving as the Chief Guest and Guest of Honour, and a certificate award ceremony recognizing the competition winners. The guest lectures will provide valuable insights into photography techniques, creativity, visual storytelling, and career opportunities in the field of photography. Souvenirs will also be presented to the distinguished guests, the Principal, the MIC, and past presidents of the Photography Club.

Photography Basics

Short, practical lessons from the club. Read a lesson, watch the video, study the photos, then try the challenge and share your best frame with us.

1

Composition

  • Switch on the grid and place your subject on a line or crossing point (rule of thirds).
  • Use leading lines such as roads, railings and shadows to pull the eye to your subject.
  • Simplify. Remove clutter, or move closer.

Try this: take five photos of one object, each from a different angle.

2

Light

  • The hour after sunrise and the hour before sunset give soft, warm light.
  • Midday sun is harsh: look for shade, or shoot with the sun behind you.
  • Window light is excellent for portraits. Turn your subject towards it.

Try this: photograph the same spot in the morning and in the evening.

3

Exposure basics

  • Wide aperture gives a blurry background; narrow keeps more in focus.
  • Fast shutter freezes motion; slow shutter blurs it.
  • Keep ISO as low as the light allows.

Try this: freeze a moving friend, then blur them on purpose.

4

Phone photography

  • A phone is a real camera. Clean the lens before you shoot.
  • Tap to focus, then slide to set the brightness.
  • Skip digital zoom. Walk closer instead.

Try this: shoot a portrait with a window beside your subject.

This week's challenge

Shadows. Find a strong shadow, and make it the subject of your photo. Send your best frame to the club.
